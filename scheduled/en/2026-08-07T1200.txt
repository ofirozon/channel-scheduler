🛑 Your hook printed a warning, and Claude ignored it. Of course it did.

A hook that exits 0 sends its stderr to the debug log and nowhere else. Claude never sees it. If you want a hook to actually stop something, exit 2.

On exit 2, Claude Code throws away stdout and feeds your stderr back to Claude as an error. For a `PreToolUse` hook that blocks the tool call outright. Any other non-zero code just prints a notice and lets the action run.

It comes down to one number. `exit 2` guards. `exit 1` decorates.

Worth it because: your "never commit straight to main" hook stops being a suggestion.

Source: code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks
