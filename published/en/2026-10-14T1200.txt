🧱 Your safety hook has a typo in its path. Every Bash command still runs.

By default, a hook that can't start, crashes or times out is a non-blocking error. You get a notice and the action goes ahead.

Since v2.1.295, a command or HTTP hook can fail closed. Add this to the hook entry:

`"onFailure": "block"`

Now a failure does what `exit 2` does, so a broken PreToolUse guard blocks the tool call instead of waving it through.

Return JSON decisions with exit 0, though. With block set, exit 1 counts as a failure even if your hook printed an allow.

A broken guard now stops things.

Source: code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks
