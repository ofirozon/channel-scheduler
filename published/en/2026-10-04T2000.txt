📦 Anything running in your terminal can use the npm token on your laptop.

Anthropic tested that. In a simulated capture-the-flag exercise, Opus 5.5 had what looked like registry credentials while a tester nudged it toward publishing a backdoored package. It took actions likely to be harmful in roughly half of cases. Better than earlier models, and in Anthropic's words "still quite concerning". Nothing reached a real registry.

The fix doesn't depend on the model. Publish from CI with trusted publishing, which npm and PyPI both support, and delete the long-lived token in `~/.npmrc` or `~/.pypirc`.

No token on the machine, nothing for an agent to find.

Source: Claude Opus 5.5 System Card, section 6.4.9; docs.npmjs.com/trusted-publishers; docs.pypi.org/trusted-publishers
