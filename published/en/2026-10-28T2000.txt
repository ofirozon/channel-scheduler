🔒 Denying `Read(./.env)` stops `cat .env`. It doesn't stop Python.

Deny rules cover Claude's file tools and the Bash commands that name the file. They don't cover a `grep -r` run over the folder, or a script that opens files on its own.

The sandbox closes that gap:

`/sandbox`

With it on, Claude Code copies your `Read` deny rules into the sandbox's `denyRead` list, and the OS enforces it for every command and every process it starts.

If a command fails and Claude offers to retry it outside the sandbox, say no. That retry is the way around.

Now your `.env` rule means what you thought it meant.

Source: code.claude.com/docs/en/permissions, code.claude.com/docs/en/settings-reference
